The One-Sentence Doctrine

You have the worse roster, so turn a 12-possession game into a 7-possession game: bleed clock every snap, take the checkdown, and make scheme conflict (RPOs, rubs, options) beat talent — one defender wrong beats five ratings points every time.

The five rules

  1. Milk the play clock under 5 on every offensive snap unless trailing late.
  2. Long drives ARE your defense. Every first down deletes 40+ seconds of their offense.
  3. No 50/50 lob throws. Your receivers lose the dice roll. Checkdowns and in-bounds completions.
  4. Add variance only at decision points: go on 4th-and-short past midfield. The sneak meter makes 4th-and-1 a skill play (check for a pinched line first).
  5. Take points early when drives stall; in the 4th quarter down 4–8, the conversion attempt usually beats the field goal.

The Script 15 — Openers as Reconnaissance

Walsh scripted openers so his brain was free to watch the defense's answers. Same here. By play 15 you must know three facts: man/zone %, blitz down and side, where his user lives. Those three facts call the rest of the game.

The 10-Branch Adjustment Tree

  1. Man confirmed → crossers, drags, wheels; motion the fast guy.
  2. Zone confirmed → high-low the flat defender; patience; checkdowns are wins.
  3. Blitzes from a known side → slide protection to it, back releases away, throw the glance behind it. One completion usually ends the blitzing.
  4. Users the middle → stop throwing crossers at him; attack flats/seams; pump the crosser, throw the dig behind him.
  5. Drops 8 all game → checkdown and draw every time, milk clock, zero shame.
  6. Shows 2-high pre-snap → confirm at the snap (safety first-steps) before anything deep. This cycle's disguises are good.
  7. Heavy box / manual pinch → stretch, toss, PA shots. Light box (≤6) → run inside, period.
  8. Inside run stuffed → don't abandon the run, change the track: counter, draw, outside zone from the same look.
  9. He spams one concept → find the ONE route beating you, take away exactly that with ONE adjustment. Never stack five adjustments.
  10. Tilting / nothing works → timeout, back to base run + PA pair. There is no branch called "improvise."

Defense: Personnel First, Then Situation

The first read is their huddle, not the down marker. Count backs and tight ends as they break: that number picks your package before down and distance says anything. Each personnel rung is ONE package with a complete four-call menu — single-high force, two-high shell, man, pressure — because one package is all you can operate in 25 seconds.

Never let 12+ personnel see a 6-DB field. If they substitute, you substitute. Short yardage, obvious pass, red zone, and goal line stay situational — they override the ladder when the chains say so.

The home package

Playbook: 4-2-5 — Ole Miss's stock defensive book, so there is nothing to switch pregame: pick Ole Miss and every call on this sheet is in your menus. Home: Nickel 3-3 Mint (3-down nickel, same geometry the old Cub home had). Pressure comes from alignment stress (mug looks), not player talent — that's why it works with a worse roster — and the coverage calls are zone/match so your corners never live on man islands.

Honest caveat: plain 4-2-5 is A-tier, not S-tier — it gives up the 3-3 Cub and the 4-3 Even 6-1 heavy front. In exchange the whole sheet works with zero pregame friction. Mint covers ~90% of what Cub did; run downs now lean on 4-2-5 Over G and 3-4 Tite.
Since patch 1.006 your package persists vs no-huddle (re-confirmed fixed in TU 2.5, July 23). Tempo can no longer wipe your personnel. The whole anti-cheese plan is legal now — and TU 2.5's timing-catch nerf shrank the green window into coverage, so playing real shells got a free buff.

Before every game (menus — zero in-game seconds)

  • Coach adjustments: Auto Flip ON, Base Align ON (same alignment every snap = disguise by sameness), Plaster ON, Smart Zones aggressive underneath, Gap Integrity Conservative, RPO keys set once you've seen their lean.
  • Set the four audibles above from Nickel 3-3 Mint.

The 5-second tempo routine

  1. Audible, don't browse. LT → one of the four. Never open the full menu vs tempo.
  2. Glance at your safeties (live bug: some shells misalign — re-audible the same play if wrong). Bug also eats macros: they can fire minus one setting, so hand-confirm the one adjustment that matters.
  3. One adjustment max: shade underneath vs quick game, over top vs shots.
  4. Pre-select your user defender — your roaming safety by default. Palms and 1 Double WR1 are the panic pair precisely because their shells stay sound while you freelance; in Tampa 2 take the hole LB instead.
Blitzing blind vs tempo is how underdogs die. Only BUCK ZONE BLITZ is acceptable blind pressure (3-deep net behind it). And timeouts are tempo-breakers: the moment tilt rises — he converts and sprints to the line — burn one. Keep two in the second half for clock; the rest exist to be spent on composure.

Bug tripwires (verified live, Aug 4)

  • Shells randomly misalign — read your own shell pre-snap; re-call if wrong.
  • Flip vs trips→bunch shift: slot corner may not travel; clicking him teleports + freezes your user ~5s. Flip early at huddle break — never click the stuck defender.
  • Macros can drop one setting — hand-confirm the adjustment that matters.
  • Shift = unlimited hot routes (their side): after any shift, assume the concept was rebuilt at the line. Play leverage, not play-art memory.

Between Plays: The 25-Second Ritual

0–5s
Log, don't feel. One word: "man," "blitz-left," "user-mid."
5–10s
On defense: personnel names the block — count backs + TEs as they break; LIVE only knows down/distance. On offense the LIVE highlight already did it. Read 2–4 options, not forty.
10–15s
Pick and commit. A decent call fully executed beats a perfect call chosen at 0:03.
15–25s
Sheet down, eyes up. Shell, box, user, leverage. Milk to ~3. Snap.

Stillness sharpens sight. The sheet exists so the decision is already made —
and the 25 seconds go to watching, not choosing.
